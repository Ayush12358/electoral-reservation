# External Claim Source Audit

**Scope.** This audit checks time-sensitive numerical and legal claims in the
Introduction and Policy Discussion of `PAPER.md` against first-party/official
sources only. It does not audit the paper's internally generated empirical
results, literature summaries, or normative recommendations. Sources were
checked on 17 July 2026.

## Source record

- [Election Commission of India, *Atlas 2024*, Annexure II](https://www.eci.gov.in/EBooks/atlas-2024/files/basic-html/page137.html): 8,360 contesting candidates in the 2024 Lok Sabha election, including 800 women (9.6%); 74 women elected (13.6%); and 2019's 8,054 contestants, 726 women (9.0%), and 78 women elected (14.4%). It also records women as 48.6% of 2024 electors.
- [Election Commission of India, General Election to Lok Sabha 2024 statistical reports](https://www.eci.gov.in/general-election-to-loksabha-2024-statistical-reports): official report index, including women-candidate participation and individual-performance tables.
- [Association for Democratic Reforms, *Women's Political Participation and Representation in India 2026*](https://adrindia.org/content/Womens-Political-Participation-and-Representation-in-India-2026): the original publication of the paper's cited March 2026 aggregation. It is a research/advocacy source rather than an official election authority; use it only with its stated coverage and exclusions.
- [Constitution (One Hundred and Sixth Amendment) Act, 2023, Gazette](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/677f65dda7c606ba1d13b8430af70555.pdf): inserts Articles 330A, 332A and 334A. It provides for seats reserved for women and, as nearly as may be, one-third of directly elected Lok Sabha and state-assembly seats. Article 334A makes the reservation provisions operative only after a delimitation undertaken after publication of relevant figures for the first census taken after the Act's commencement.
- [S.O. 1922(E), Gazette notification of 16 April 2026](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/271834.pdf): appoints 16 April 2026 as the date on which the 106th Amendment Act came into force.
- [Lok Sabha, Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-first Amendment) Bill, 2026, as introduced](https://sansad.in/getFile/BillsTexts/LSBillTexts/Asintroduced/AS%20INTRO416202612944PM.pdf?source=legislation): the proposed replacement Article 334A would make women-seat reservation operative after a delimitation exercise, without the 106th Amendment's first-post-commencement-census condition. The bill's statement of objects says the next census and consequent delimitation would take considerable time.
- [Lok Sabha bill register](https://sansad.in/ls/legislation/bills): records Bill No. 107 of 2026, introduced 16 April 2026, as **NEGATIVED** in the Lok Sabha.
- [Ministry of Home Affairs, Lok Sabha answer of 2 December 2025](https://www.mha.gov.in/MHA1/Par2017/pdfs/par2025-pdfs/LS02122025/258.pdf): says the Government decided to conduct Census 2027 in two phases: houselisting and housing census from April to September 2026, then population enumeration in February 2027. This is a planned/ongoing operation, not published census figures or completed delimitation.
- [Inter-Parliamentary Union / UN Women, 11 March 2026 release](https://www.ipu.org/news/press-releases/2026-03/only-1-in-7-countries-led-woman-global-political-power-remains-dominated-men): reports that women held 27.5% of parliamentary seats worldwide as of 1 January 2026.
- [IPU Parline monthly data, April 2026](https://data.ipu.org/women-averages/?date_month=04&date_year=2026): reports a live, revision-prone all-chamber figure of 27.3%, and explains that the data are updated from national parliaments.

## Findings and corrections

| Location / manuscript claim | Finding | Recommended correction |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction: “800 of 8,360 candidates (9.6%) were women, and just 74 women were elected — 13.6% … decline from 14.4% in 2019.” | **Supported, with one source citation repair.** ECI directly confirms 800 female contestants and the 9.6%, 13.6%, and 14.4% shares. The percentage arithmetic supports 8,360 total candidates and 74 of 543 elected members (13.63%). | Cite ECI's 2024 statistical report/Atlas directly instead of ADR/PRS for this sentence. Retain the numbers. |
| Introduction: “Across all current Lok Sabha and State/UT Assembly elections, women constitute approximately 10% of candidates and 10% of legislators (ADR 2026).” | **Supported only as a clearly attributed ADR research snapshot, not an official live count.** ADR's own March 2026 publication reports 5,095 women among 51,708 candidates (10.0%) and 464 women among 4,666 MPs/MLAs (10.0%), and excludes subsequent by-elections. | Replace the broad “all current” wording with: “In ADR's March 2026 main-election snapshot (excluding subsequent by-elections), women were 5,095 of 51,708 candidates and 464 of 4,666 MPs/MLAs (10.0% each).” Preserve the ADR citation and its scope. |
| Introduction: “The global average for women in national parliaments is 27.5% (IPU 2026).” | **Supported only as a dated snapshot.** IPU's 11 March release gives 27.5% as of 1 January 2026; its live April series is 27.3% across all chambers. | Write “As of 1 January 2026, IPU reported that women held 27.5% of parliamentary seats worldwide,” and cite the IPU release. This preserves the intended comparison without implying a timeless average. |
| Introduction: “The passage of the Women's Reservation Act … in 2023, mandating 33% reservation for women in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies …” | **Substantively supported but legally imprecise.** The formal enactment is the Constitution (One Hundred and Sixth Amendment) Act, 2023; Articles 330A and 332A use “as nearly as may be, one-third,” not an exact 33%, and also cover the Delhi Assembly. | Use: “The Constitution (One Hundred and Sixth Amendment) Act, 2023 (popularly, the Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam) provides for reservation of, as nearly as may be, one-third of directly elected seats in the Lok Sabha and state legislative assemblies [and Delhi].” |
| Introduction: “the law remains unimplemented as of mid-2026 because it requires a census and delimitation exercise that have not occurred.” | **Needs legal-status and census-status precision.** The Act itself came into force on 16 April 2026. Article 334A nevertheless prevents the *seat-reservation provisions* from becoming operative until a purpose-specific delimitation after publication of the relevant figures for the first census taken after commencement. Census 2027 operations are underway, but the required figures and delimitation have not been completed. | Replace “the law remains unimplemented” with “the Act is in force, but its seat-reservation provisions are not yet operative under Article 334A.” State the two statutory conditions exactly; do not say that a census process has not occurred. |
| Introduction: “The Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026, which would have delinked reservation from the census, was defeated in the Lok Sabha.” | **Partly supported; scope is overstated.** The official bill register records it as negatived. Its proposed Article 334A would remove the 106th Amendment's first-post-commencement-census condition, but would still require a delimitation exercise; other proposed constitutional provisions continue to refer to a census. | Write: “The Lok Sabha negatived the Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-first Amendment) Bill, 2026. Its proposed Article 334A would have removed the 106th Amendment's first-post-commencement-census condition for women-seat reservation, while retaining a delimitation requirement.” |
| Introduction: “Women’s representation is therefore governed entirely by party nomination behavior — with no legal quota at the national level.” | **Contradicted as phrased.** A national constitutional quota is legally enacted and in force; it is not yet operative for allocating seats. Party nomination is not the only legal determinant of women’s representation, although it is the operative route while no seats have been designated. | Write: “Until women-reserved seats become operative, representation in ordinary elections depends on party nomination and electoral competition; the constitutional women’s quota exists but has not yet been operationalized into designated seats.” |
| §6.2 table: “No party at 33%.” | **Not verified by official sources in this audit.** It appears to rely on an ADR analysis rather than ECI's official results. It is also underspecified (which election universe, parties, and denominator). | Add a dated data source and denominator, qualify as ADR's result, or remove from the external-evidence table. |
| §6.2 table: `13.6% / ~47% = 0.29` population-share benchmark. | **Stale if it purports to use the 2024 electorate.** ECI's Atlas gives women as 48.6% of 2024 Lok Sabha electors, not approximately 47%; 13.6 / 48.6 = 0.280. The estimate may be separately defensible with a different named population source, but that source is absent. | Either cite and use an explicitly specified population denominator, or, for 2024 electors, revise to `13.6% / 48.6% = 0.28`. |
| §6.3: “The Women's Reservation Act came into force on April 16, 2026.” | **Supported.** S.O. 1922(E) appoints 16 April 2026. | Cite the Gazette notification, not news reporting. |
| §6.3: “Under current law, implementation may be delayed until 2034.” | **Not supported by the controlling legal text.** Article 334A specifies prerequisites, not an implementation year. A 2034 date is a forecast contingent on future census, delimitation, and election timing. | Delete the date, or label it explicitly as a sourced scenario/forecast and explain its assumptions. The legal statement should stop at the statutory prerequisites. |
| §6.3: “without institutional correction, we can directly observe whether parties will voluntarily nominate enough women…” | **Overbroad legal characterization.** The constitutional correction is in force but not yet operative; ordinary contests remain unreserved while the trigger has not occurred. | Say “while the constitutionally enacted seat reservation is not yet operative…” This avoids implying repeal or legal absence. |

## Bottom line

The 2024 election counts, the dated IPU benchmark, the 16 April 2026 commencement, and the defeat of Bill No. 107 are supportable from first-party sources. The manuscript needs its sharpest correction on legal status: the 106th Amendment is **in force**, while Article 334A makes its women-seat reservation provisions **not yet operative**. The claimed 2034 date has no official legal basis. The ADR all-election snapshot must be explicitly attributed and bounded, and the “no party at 33%” claim needs a reproducible data definition.
